================================================================================
SEC FILINGS AND EDGAR
================================================================================

PURPOSE

This section explains the SEC's EDGAR system and common SEC filings used to
research publicly traded companies.

SEC filings can provide information about a company's financial performance,
business, risks, management, ownership, and important corporate events.

================================================================================
1. WHAT IS THE SEC?
================================================================================

DEFINITION

The SEC is the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

The SEC is a U.S. federal agency responsible for administering and enforcing
federal securities laws and overseeing important parts of U.S. securities
markets.

Public companies are required to make various disclosures and filings with
the SEC.

These filings allow investors and the public to access important information
about publicly traded companies.

RELATED TERMS

SEC, securities regulation, public company, disclosure, EDGAR

================================================================================
2. WHAT IS EDGAR?
================================================================================

DEFINITION

EDGAR is the SEC's Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval system.

EDGAR provides free public access to millions of documents filed with the SEC.

Investors can use EDGAR to research companies and retrieve documents including:

- Registration statements
- Annual reports
- Quarterly reports
- Current reports
- Prospectuses
- Ownership filings
- Other SEC filings

Users can search for companies using information such as company names or
ticker symbols.

WHY EDGAR MATTERS

Financial websites often summarize company information.

EDGAR allows users to access the filings submitted to the SEC themselves.

SAMPLE Q&A

Q: What is EDGAR?

A: EDGAR is the SEC's electronic filing database. It provides free public
access to company filings such as 10-K annual reports, 10-Q quarterly reports,
8-K current reports, and registration statements.

================================================================================
3. FORM 10-K
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Form 10-K is an annual report filed by public companies with the SEC.

It provides detailed information about the company's business and financial
condition.

A 10-K can contain information about:

- The company's business
- Financial statements
- Company risks
- Management's discussion of financial results
- Legal proceedings
- Company operations
- Other required disclosures

WHY IT MATTERS

A 10-K is one of the most important sources for researching a public company's
business and financial condition.

Unlike a short company overview from a financial website, a 10-K contains
detailed disclosures filed with the SEC.

RELATED TERMS

annual report, SEC filing, financial statements, MD&A, risk factors, EDGAR

================================================================================
4. FORM 10-Q
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Form 10-Q is a quarterly report filed by public companies with the SEC.

It provides updated information about the company's financial position and
operations during the reporting period.

A 10-Q generally contains:

- Quarterly financial statements
- Information about company operations
- Management discussion
- Updates to certain risks and disclosures

KEY POINT

A 10-Q provides more frequent updates than a 10-K.

The 10-K provides annual information, while the 10-Q provides information for
quarterly reporting periods.

RELATED TERMS

quarterly report, earnings, financial statements, 10-K, EDGAR

================================================================================
5. 10-K VS. 10-Q
================================================================================

10-K

- Annual filing
- Provides detailed yearly information
- Contains extensive information about the company's business
- Contains annual financial information and disclosures

10-Q

- Quarterly filing
- Provides more frequent financial updates
- Covers recent quarterly performance and developments

SIMPLE EXPLANATION

A 10-K is the major annual company filing.

A 10-Q provides quarterly updates between annual reports.

SAMPLE Q&A

Q: What's the difference between a 10-K and a 10-Q?

A: A 10-K is an annual SEC filing containing detailed information about a
company's business and financial condition. A 10-Q is a quarterly SEC filing
that provides more frequent updates about financial performance and operations.

================================================================================
6. FORM 8-K
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Form 8-K is used by public companies to report certain important corporate
events.

Unlike a 10-K or 10-Q, an 8-K is not primarily a regularly scheduled annual or
quarterly financial report.

Instead, it reports certain events that occur between regular reporting periods.

An 8-K can therefore provide information about recent corporate developments.

WHY IT MATTERS

If an investor wants to know whether an important corporate event has recently
been disclosed, checking recent 8-K filings can be useful.

RELATED TERMS

current report, material event, corporate event, SEC disclosure

================================================================================
7. 10-K VS. 10-Q VS. 8-K
================================================================================

10-K:
Annual report containing detailed information about the company.

10-Q:
Quarterly report providing more frequent financial and operational updates.

8-K:
Current report used to report certain important corporate events.

EASY WAY TO REMEMBER

10-K = annual

10-Q = quarterly

8-K = important current events

SAMPLE Q&A

Q: Which SEC filing should I look at for annual financial information?

A: The 10-K is the primary annual filing.

Q: Which filing gives quarterly updates?

A: The 10-Q.

Q: Which filing can tell me about a recent important corporate event?

A: An 8-K may report certain important corporate events.

================================================================================
8. FORM S-1
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Form S-1 is a registration statement commonly associated with companies
registering securities for a public offering.

An S-1 can provide extensive information about a company before its securities
are offered publicly.

Information can include:

- The company's business
- Financial information
- Risks
- Management
- Intended use of offering proceeds
- Information concerning the securities offering

WHY IT MATTERS

An S-1 can be especially useful when researching a company preparing for an
initial public offering.

RELATED TERMS

IPO, registration statement, prospectus, offering, SEC

================================================================================
9. PROSPECTUS
================================================================================

DEFINITION

A prospectus is a disclosure document associated with a securities offering.

It provides information investors can use to learn about the issuer and the
offering.

Information can include:

- Company information
- Financial information
- Risk factors
- Offering terms
- Use of proceeds

RELATED TERMS

IPO, securities offering, S-1, registration statement

================================================================================
10. FORM 4
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Form 4 is associated with reporting certain transactions involving company
insiders and company securities.

These filings can provide information about transactions involving shares or
other company securities by covered insiders.

IMPORTANT

An insider transaction should not automatically be interpreted as evidence
that a stock will rise or fall.

Transactions can occur for many reasons.

The filing provides information about the reported transaction. Investors
should avoid assuming a motive that is not disclosed.

RELATED TERMS

insider transaction, beneficial ownership, executive, director

================================================================================
11. FORMS 3, 4, AND 5
================================================================================

Forms 3, 4, and 5 are associated with reporting beneficial ownership and
transactions involving certain company insiders.

A simplified distinction is:

Form 3:
Initial ownership reporting for covered insiders.

Form 4:
Reports certain changes in ownership.

Form 5:
Used for certain ownership information that is reported on an annual basis.

These filings can help researchers examine disclosed insider ownership and
transactions.

RELATED TERMS

insider ownership, beneficial ownership, Form 4

================================================================================
12. FORM 13F
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Form 13F is associated with reports filed by certain institutional investment
managers concerning specified securities holdings.

Researchers sometimes use 13F filings to examine reported institutional
holdings.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION

A 13F should not be treated as a complete real-time picture of everything an
investment manager owns or is currently doing.

The filing reflects information required by the reporting framework.

Therefore:

"What did this institution report holding?"

is different from:

"What does this institution own at this exact moment?"

RELATED TERMS

institutional investor, holdings, investment manager, SEC filing

================================================================================
13. SCHEDULE 13D
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Schedule 13D is a beneficial-ownership filing associated with certain
significant ownership positions in publicly traded securities.

It can provide information about:

- The reporting person
- The ownership position
- Information required about the acquisition
- Certain intentions or purposes associated with the position

These filings can be useful when researching significant shareholders.

RELATED TERMS

beneficial ownership, shareholder, SEC filing, Schedule 13G

================================================================================
14. SCHEDULE 13G
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Schedule 13G is another beneficial-ownership filing associated with certain
significant ownership positions.

Schedule 13D and Schedule 13G relate to beneficial ownership but apply under
different reporting circumstances.

When researching ownership, investors should examine the actual filing rather
than assuming that all significant shareholders file the same form.

RELATED TERMS

13D, beneficial ownership, shareholder

================================================================================
15. RISK FACTORS
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Public-company filings can contain a section describing risks associated with
the company and its business.

Risk factors might relate to areas such as:

- Business operations
- Competition
- Financial conditions
- Regulation
- Technology
- Supply chains
- Customers
- Economic conditions

The particular risks vary by company.

WHY IT MATTERS

Risk-factor disclosures can help investors understand problems that management
identifies as potentially important to the company.

IMPORTANT

A risk being disclosed does not mean that the risk will definitely occur.

Likewise, investors should not assume that two companies have identical risks.

================================================================================
16. MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS (MD&A)
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Management's Discussion and Analysis, commonly called MD&A, is a section of
company filings in which management discusses aspects of the company's
financial condition and operating results.

The section helps provide context around financial information.

It can help answer questions such as:

- What contributed to changes in revenue?
- What contributed to changes in expenses?
- What business conditions affected the company?
- How does management explain changes in financial performance?

WHY IT MATTERS

Financial statements show financial figures.

MD&A can provide management's explanation and discussion of those figures.

IMPORTANT

Management commentary should be considered alongside the company's financial
statements and other disclosures.

RELATED TERMS

management commentary, 10-K, 10-Q, financial statements

================================================================================
17. FINANCIAL STATEMENTS IN SEC FILINGS
================================================================================

SEC filings can contain important financial statements.

Three major financial statements are:

1. Income statement
2. Balance sheet
3. Cash flow statement

INCOME STATEMENT

Shows information about company revenue, expenses, and profit or loss over a
period.

BALANCE SHEET

Shows assets, liabilities, and shareholders' equity at a point in time.

CASH FLOW STATEMENT

Shows cash flows associated with operating, investing, and financing
activities.

Together, these statements provide different views of the company's financial
condition and performance.

RELATED TERMS

income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement, financial reporting

================================================================================
18. HOW TO READ A 10-K
================================================================================

A beginner does not necessarily need to read a 10-K from the first page to the
last page.

Useful areas to examine include:

1. Business

Learn what the company does and how the company describes its operations.

2. Risk Factors

Identify important risks disclosed by the company.

3. MD&A

Read management's discussion of operating results and financial condition.

4. Financial Statements

Review:

- Revenue
- Expenses
- Profit
- Assets
- Liabilities
- Cash flow

5. Notes to Financial Statements

The notes can provide important additional explanation about financial
statement items.

A financial figure should not always be interpreted in isolation. Context from
the rest of the filing can matter.

================================================================================
19. MATERIAL AND CORPORATE EVENTS
================================================================================

Companies sometimes experience important developments between normal quarterly
or annual reporting periods.

Certain events may trigger current reporting through Form 8-K.

Therefore, someone researching a company should not necessarily look only at
the most recent 10-K.

Recent filings can provide newer information.

SAMPLE Q&A

Q: Where should I look for recent company events?

A: Check the company's recent SEC filings. Form 8-K is used to report certain
important current corporate events.

================================================================================
20. INSIDER TRANSACTIONS
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Insider transactions refer to reported transactions involving certain people
associated with a company and the company's securities.

SEC ownership filings can provide information about these transactions.

IMPORTANT

"Insider transaction" does not automatically mean illegal insider trading.

The existence of a reported transaction alone also does not tell you why the
transaction happened.

Researchers should distinguish:

reported insider ownership transactions

from:

illegal trading based on material nonpublic information.

These are not the same concept.

================================================================================
21. HOW EDGAR FITS INTO COMPANY RESEARCH
================================================================================

EDGAR can serve as a primary source when researching publicly traded companies.

For example:

Want annual company information?
Look for a 10-K.

Want a quarterly update?
Look for a 10-Q.

Want information about certain recent corporate events?
Look for an 8-K.

Researching an IPO?
Look for the registration statement and prospectus, commonly including an S-1.

Researching certain insider transactions?
Look at ownership filings such as Form 4.

Researching institutional holdings?
Certain Form 13F filings may be relevant.

Researching significant beneficial ownership?
Schedule 13D or 13G filings may be relevant.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
================================================================================

"A 10-K is just an earnings report."

No. A 10-K is a detailed annual SEC filing containing considerably more
information than headline earnings figures.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"10-K and 10-Q mean the same thing."

No. A 10-K is an annual filing, while a 10-Q provides quarterly information.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"8-K means eight thousand."

No. 8-K is the name of an SEC form used for current reporting of certain
corporate events.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"An SEC filing tells me whether I should buy the stock."

No. SEC filings provide information. They do not determine whether a particular
investment is suitable for an investor.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Form 4 activity automatically means the stock is going up or down."

No. A reported transaction does not by itself establish what will happen to a
stock price.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"EDGAR is a stock-data website."

EDGAR is the SEC's electronic filing system. It provides access to official
documents submitted through the SEC filing system.
