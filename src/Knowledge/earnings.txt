================================================================================
EARNINGS AND EARNINGS REPORTS
================================================================================

PURPOSE

This section explains company earnings, earnings reports, earnings per share,
analyst expectations, earnings beats and misses, company guidance, earnings
calls, and why stock prices often move after earnings announcements.

================================================================================
1. WHAT ARE EARNINGS?
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Earnings generally refer to a company's profit over a particular period.

Revenue represents money generated from a company's business activities before
all expenses are subtracted.

Earnings represent what remains after applicable costs and expenses are
accounted for.

KEY POINTS

- Revenue and earnings are not the same thing.
- A company can have growing revenue while its earnings decline.
- Earnings can be positive or negative.
- Negative earnings mean the company recorded a net loss for the period.
- Investors often compare earnings across different periods to understand how
  profitability is changing.
- Earnings alone do not provide a complete picture of a company's financial
  condition.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A company generates:

Revenue: $10 million
Total expenses: $8 million

Simplified earnings:

$10 million - $8 million = $2 million

The company generated $10 million in revenue and $2 million in earnings.

RELATED TERMS

net income, profit, loss, revenue, expenses, EPS, profitability

================================================================================
2. EARNINGS REPORTS
================================================================================

DEFINITION

An earnings report provides information about a company's financial performance
over a particular reporting period.

Public companies regularly provide financial information through filings and
other earnings-related materials.

An earnings report can help investors understand:

- Revenue
- Profit or loss
- Earnings per share
- Expenses
- Cash flow
- Financial position
- Business performance
- Management commentary

Quarterly SEC reports are generally filed using Form 10-Q.

Annual reports are generally filed using Form 10-K.

KEY POINTS

An earnings announcement may involve several different materials, including:

- Earnings press release
- SEC filing
- Financial statements
- Investor presentation
- Earnings call
- Management commentary

The headline revenue and EPS numbers therefore represent only part of the
information available when a company reports results.

RELATED TERMS

10-Q, 10-K, income statement, financial statements, earnings call

================================================================================
3. EARNINGS SEASON
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Earnings season is an informal term for periods when many publicly traded
companies release their quarterly financial results.

KEY POINTS

During earnings season investors may receive new information about:

- Company revenue
- Earnings
- Earnings per share
- Profit margins
- Cash flow
- Business segments
- Management expectations

Stocks can experience significant price movement around earnings announcements
because the reports provide new information about company performance and
expectations.

RELATED TERMS

quarterly results, earnings report, earnings call, EPS, guidance

================================================================================
4. EARNINGS PER SHARE (EPS)
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Earnings per share, or EPS, measures how much of a company's earnings is
attributable to each share of common stock.

A simplified version of EPS is:

EPS = Net Income / Weighted Average Shares Outstanding

Actual accounting calculations can include additional adjustments.

EXAMPLE

Suppose a company earns:

Net income = $100 million

and has:

Weighted average shares = 50 million

Simplified EPS:

$100 million / 50 million = $2.00 EPS

KEY POINTS

- EPS allows earnings to be expressed on a per-share basis.
- EPS can increase because profit increased.
- EPS can also increase when the number of shares decreases.
- Share buybacks can therefore affect EPS.
- Issuing additional shares can affect EPS.
- EPS should not automatically be interpreted as company growth without
  investigating what caused the change.

RELATED TERMS

net income, shares outstanding, diluted EPS, share buyback, dilution

================================================================================
5. BASIC VS. DILUTED EPS
================================================================================

BASIC EPS

Basic EPS calculates earnings per share using the weighted average number of
common shares actually outstanding during the period.

DILUTED EPS

Diluted EPS considers the potential effect of securities or compensation that
could increase the number of common shares.

Potential dilution can arise from instruments such as:

- Stock options
- Restricted stock
- Convertible securities

WHY IT MATTERS

Diluted EPS attempts to show what EPS could look like after considering
potential additional shares.

As a result, diluted EPS can be lower than basic EPS.

SIMPLE EXPLANATION

Basic EPS:
Uses the current weighted average common share count.

Diluted EPS:
Also considers potential shares that could dilute existing shareholders.

RELATED TERMS

dilution, stock options, convertible securities, weighted average shares

================================================================================
6. REVENUE VS. EARNINGS
================================================================================

REVENUE

Revenue is money generated from the company's business activities before all
expenses are deducted.

EARNINGS

Earnings represent profit after applicable costs and expenses.

EXAMPLE

Company:

Revenue = $1 billion
Expenses = $900 million
Earnings = $100 million

The company generated much more revenue than earnings because operating the
business involved substantial expenses.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A company's revenue can rise while earnings fall.

Example:

Previous year:

Revenue = $1 billion
Expenses = $800 million
Earnings = $200 million

Current year:

Revenue = $1.2 billion
Expenses = $1.1 billion
Earnings = $100 million

Revenue increased 20%, but earnings declined because expenses increased even
more.

SAMPLE Q&A

Q: Is revenue the same as earnings?

A: No. Revenue represents money generated by the business before all expenses
are deducted. Earnings represent the profit remaining after applicable
expenses.

================================================================================
7. EARNINGS ESTIMATES
================================================================================

DEFINITION

An earnings estimate is a forecast of a company's expected earnings for a
future reporting period.

Analysts may publish estimates for financial metrics including:

- EPS
- Revenue
- Growth
- Other company-specific metrics

Financial data providers may combine analyst forecasts into a consensus
estimate.

KEY POINT

An estimate is a prediction, not a guaranteed result.

When actual results are released, investors often compare those results against
expectations.

RELATED TERMS

analyst estimate, consensus estimate, expectations, EPS

================================================================================
8. EARNINGS BEAT
================================================================================

DEFINITION

An earnings beat occurs when a company's reported earnings exceed the
expectation being used for comparison, commonly an analyst consensus estimate.

EXAMPLE

Expected EPS:

$1.20

Reported EPS:

$1.35

The company exceeded the EPS estimate by:

$0.15

This would generally be described as an earnings beat relative to that estimate.

IMPORTANT

An earnings beat does not guarantee that the stock price will rise.

The market may also react to:

- Revenue
- Guidance
- Profit margins
- Cash flow
- Business segment performance
- Previously priced-in expectations
- Management commentary

RELATED TERMS

earnings surprise, analyst estimate, EPS, consensus estimate

================================================================================
9. EARNINGS MISS
================================================================================

DEFINITION

An earnings miss occurs when reported earnings are below the expectation being
used for comparison.

EXAMPLE

Expected EPS:

$2.00

Actual EPS:

$1.80

The company missed that EPS estimate by:

$0.20

KEY POINT

An earnings miss does not automatically mean the company's entire business is
performing poorly.

Investors typically consider the broader financial results and expectations.

================================================================================
10. REVENUE BEAT AND REVENUE MISS
================================================================================

A revenue beat occurs when reported revenue exceeds the revenue estimate being
used for comparison.

A revenue miss occurs when reported revenue is below that estimate.

EXAMPLE

Expected revenue:

$5 billion

Reported revenue:

$5.3 billion

The result represents a $300 million revenue beat relative to that estimate.

IMPORTANT

Revenue and earnings expectations are separate.

A company can:

- Beat revenue and beat earnings.
- Beat revenue but miss earnings.
- Miss revenue but beat earnings.
- Miss both.

This is one reason an earnings announcement cannot always be summarized using
only one number.

================================================================================
11. EARNINGS SURPRISES
================================================================================

DEFINITION

An earnings surprise is the difference between reported earnings and the
earnings expectation used for comparison.

Positive surprise:

Actual earnings exceed expectations.

Negative surprise:

Actual earnings fall below expectations.

EXAMPLE

Expected EPS = $1.00

Actual EPS = $1.10

Surprise = +$0.10 per share

KEY POINT

Markets often respond not just to whether a company made money, but to how new
information compares with expectations.

================================================================================
12. COMPANY GUIDANCE
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Company guidance refers to forward-looking expectations management provides
about future company performance.

Guidance can involve expectations about metrics such as:

- Revenue
- Earnings
- Expenses
- Profit margins
- Capital expenditures
- Other company-specific operating metrics

IMPORTANT

Companies are not necessarily required to provide investors with projections of
future operating results.

Companies that do provide guidance may revise, withdraw, or discontinue
guidance.

GUIDANCE VS. RESULTS

Results describe what already happened.

Guidance discusses management's expectations about the future.

This distinction is important because markets consider both historical
performance and expected future performance.

================================================================================
13. ANALYST ESTIMATES
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Analyst estimates are forecasts produced by financial analysts about future
company performance.

Analysts may estimate figures including:

- Revenue
- EPS
- Profitability
- Company-specific operating metrics

Multiple analyst forecasts may be combined to create a consensus estimate.

KEY POINTS

- Analyst estimates are forecasts.
- Analysts can be wrong.
- Analysts can revise estimates as new information becomes available.
- Different analysts may have different forecasts for the same company.

RELATED TERMS

consensus estimate, price target, earnings estimate, expectations

================================================================================
14. EARNINGS CALLS
================================================================================

DEFINITION

An earnings call is a presentation or conference call conducted around a
company's earnings results.

Management typically discusses company performance and provides additional
context about the results.

Earnings calls can also include questions from analysts.

INFORMATION DISCUSSED MAY INCLUDE

- Financial results
- Business performance
- Strategy
- Industry conditions
- Guidance
- Risks
- Operational developments

WHY THEY MATTER

Financial statements provide numbers.

Management discussion can provide additional explanation about what contributed
to those numbers.

However, management commentary should still be evaluated alongside the
company's actual financial disclosures.

================================================================================
15. YEAR-OVER-YEAR (YOY) GROWTH
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Year-over-year growth compares a financial metric with the corresponding period
one year earlier.

FORMULA

YOY Growth =
(Current Period - Prior-Year Period) / Prior-Year Period x 100

EXAMPLE

Revenue this year's quarter:

$120 million

Revenue same quarter last year:

$100 million

YOY growth:

($120M - $100M) / $100M x 100 = 20%

WHY USE YOY?

Comparing the same period across years can help account for businesses with
seasonal patterns.

RELATED TERMS

growth rate, revenue growth, earnings growth, quarter

================================================================================
16. QUARTER-OVER-QUARTER (QOQ) GROWTH
================================================================================

DEFINITION

Quarter-over-quarter growth compares a financial metric with the immediately
preceding quarter.

FORMULA

QOQ Growth =
(Current Quarter - Previous Quarter) / Previous Quarter x 100

EXAMPLE

Previous-quarter revenue:

$100 million

Current-quarter revenue:

$110 million

QOQ growth:

($110M - $100M) / $100M x 100 = 10%

YOY VS. QOQ

YOY:
Compares with the corresponding period one year earlier.

QOQ:
Compares with the immediately preceding quarter.

Both comparisons can provide useful but different information about growth.

================================================================================
17. FISCAL QUARTER
================================================================================

DEFINITION

A fiscal quarter is one of four periods within a company's fiscal year.

They are commonly labeled:

Q1
Q2
Q3
Q4

Companies use quarterly periods to report financial performance.

KEY POINT

A company's fiscal quarters do not necessarily align with the standard
January-through-December calendar.

Therefore, "Q1" should not automatically be assumed to mean January through
March without checking the company's fiscal calendar.

================================================================================
18. FISCAL YEAR VS. CALENDAR YEAR
================================================================================

CALENDAR YEAR

A calendar year runs:

January 1 through December 31.

FISCAL YEAR

A fiscal year is the 12-month accounting period a company uses for financial
reporting.

A company's fiscal year does not necessarily match the calendar year.

WHY IT MATTERS

When comparing financial periods, investors should verify which fiscal periods
the company is reporting.

Two companies can identify periods differently if their fiscal years end at
different times.

RELATED TERMS

fiscal quarter, annual report, 10-K, Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4

================================================================================
19. WHY STOCKS MOVE AFTER EARNINGS
================================================================================

Earnings announcements provide new information about a company.

Market participants can react to information including:

- Reported revenue
- Reported earnings
- Earnings per share
- Growth rates
- Profitability
- Cash flow
- Management guidance
- Business trends
- Management commentary
- Differences between actual results and expectations

The stock price response therefore depends on more than whether the company
reported a profit.

EXPECTATIONS MATTER

Suppose investors expected extraordinarily strong growth.

The company then reports good growth, but weaker growth than expected.

The financial result can be positive while the market's reaction is negative
because expectations were higher.

Likewise, a company can report declining results but still experience a positive
market reaction if results are better than what investors expected.

KEY IDEA

Markets react to new information relative to existing expectations, not merely
whether a company's headline numbers appear positive or negative.

================================================================================
20. WHY CAN A STOCK FALL AFTER BEATING EARNINGS?
================================================================================

A company beating an earnings estimate does NOT guarantee its stock price will
increase.

Possible reasons include:

1. REVENUE MISSED EXPECTATIONS

A company might beat its EPS estimate while reporting weaker-than-expected
revenue.

2. GUIDANCE DISAPPOINTED

Past-quarter results may be strong while management provides weaker expectations
for future periods.

3. OTHER FINANCIAL METRICS WEAKENED

Investors may focus on information such as:

- Profit margins
- Cash flow
- Expenses
- Business segment performance
- Customer or operating metrics relevant to the company

4. EXPECTATIONS WERE ALREADY VERY HIGH

Investors may have expected results substantially better than the published
consensus estimate.

Merely beating the consensus estimate might therefore not satisfy market
expectations.

5. MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY PROVIDED NEW INFORMATION

Information shared in the earnings materials or earnings call can alter
expectations about the company's future.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Suppose:

Expected EPS = $2.00
Actual EPS = $2.15

The company beat the EPS estimate.

But suppose management also communicates expectations for slower future
revenue growth.

Investors could lower their expectations about future company performance,
potentially resulting in a lower stock price despite the EPS beat.

IMPORTANT

A stock's reaction to earnings cannot be predicted from an earnings beat or
miss alone.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
================================================================================

"Earnings and revenue are the same thing."

No. Revenue generally represents money generated before all expenses, while
earnings represent profit after applicable expenses.

----------------------------------------

"If a company beats earnings, its stock will rise."

Not necessarily. Investors respond to many pieces of information and to how
those results compare with expectations.

----------------------------------------

"If a company reports positive earnings, the quarter was automatically good."

Not necessarily. Investors may compare earnings with previous periods,
expectations, revenue, cash flow, guidance, and other company-specific
information.

----------------------------------------

"Higher EPS always means the underlying business improved."

Not necessarily. EPS depends on both earnings and the number of shares used in
its calculation. Share-count changes can therefore affect EPS.

----------------------------------------

"Analyst estimates tell us what the company will earn."

No. Estimates are forecasts and can differ from actual results.
